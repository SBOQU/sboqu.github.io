// Modules

What's Inside

Eight self-contained modules, each solving a distinct challenge of designing a precision cycloidal reducer for an industrial robot joint.

01
Overview

Target: The shoulder J2 of a KUKA KR 10. Establishes the two-stage 10x10 architecture, the full kinematic chain, and frames the design against the industrial reference, the Nabtesco RV precision reducer.

Two-Stage Layout Industrial Target Nabtesco Benchmark
02
Cycloidal Profile Generation

Builds the disk geometry from the curtate epicycloid equations. Tunes the curtate coefficient K₁, computes the profile curvature radius, enforces the non-undercut condition with a direct curvature check, and calibrates the lobe to avoid sharp cusps.

Curtate Epicycloid K₁ Tuning Non-Undercut
03
Mechanical Contacts

Verifies both critical contacts, roller against profile and pin against bore, on two fronts at once: Hertzian contact pressure against admissible limits, and Pan-Hamrock EHL film thickness through the λ ratio to confirm the lubrication regime.

Hertzian Stress Pan-Hamrock λ Ratio
04
Shaft Design & Von Mises

Sizes the two eccentric shafts, one per stage, under combined bending and torsion. Propagates the sizing torque back through each stage and rounds the minimum diameters up to standard values compatible with the bearing catalog.

Von Mises Case-Hardened Steel Standard Diameters
05
Rotordynamics & Unbalance

The input shaft spins at 4000 rpm carrying an orbiting cycloidal disc. The residual unbalance force F = m e ω² is quantified, the first critical speed is estimated in closed form at about 75 times the operating speed, so no FE model is needed. The effect of the unbalance on the life of the eccentric bearing is then computed.

Rotordynamics Critical Speed Bearing Life
06
Isostatic Architecture & Bearings

Makes the assembly mountable. Each output pin gets a clearance equal to the orbit of the disc, so the pins stop fighting each other. The overturning moment is carried by a single SKF four point contact bearing, the only design that fits both the 155 mm envelope and the 40 mm axial budget.

Functional Clearance 4 Point Contact ISO 281 SKF Catalog
07
Disc Material & Ashby

With no fuse element in the drivetrain, the disc has to absorb overloads on its own. Ashby charts and elimination filters narrow the database down to seven steels, then a weighted score, checked by a sensitivity study on the weights, locks the disc material.

Ashby Method Weighted Scoring Sensitivity Check
08
Constrained Optimization

The meta-tool that drives everything else. A 14-dimensional design vector (5 integers + 9 continuous) is minimized for total disk mass under 13 penalized geometric, contact, tribological and life constraints, solved by differential evolution with simultaneous catalog bearing selection.

Differential Evolution 14 Variables Penalty Function Mass Minimization

// Design Philosophy

Analytical, Tribological, Dynamic, All Cross Checked

Instead of trusting a single static criterion, every component is checked on three planes at once: geometry, tribology and dynamics. The whole design is closed by one optimization on disc mass under hard constraints, rather than by hand iteration, and every bearing comes from a real industrial catalog.

  • Analytical Profile & Contact Profile generated from first principles, with Hertz pressure and EHL film thickness checked simultaneously on both contact families.
  • Rotordynamic Verification Residual unbalance of the orbiting disc quantified, and first critical speed estimated in closed form at about 75 times the operating speed.
  • Isostatic & Manufacturable Architecture designed to be mountable, with a functional pin clearance and a single moment bearing on the output, not just for raw performance.
  • Optimization-Driven Selection Disc material picked with the Ashby method, bearings chosen from real SKF references, and disc mass minimized by differential evolution.
